Core Structures for DSE Reading & Writing

These are the structures that make sentences long and “difficult”. Once you can see them, you can chunk the meaning.

DEFINING (essential information)

The students who finished early could leave.

→ Identifies which students. No commas.

NON-DEFINING (extra information)

My brother, who lives in Canada, is a doctor.

→ Extra fact. Needs commas. Cannot use “that”.

Key recognition tips for students:

  • Look for who / which / that / whose / where / when right after a noun.
  • If the clause can be removed and the sentence still makes sense → non-defining (extra).
  • If removing it changes the meaning or makes the noun unclear → defining (essential).
  • In DSE reading, long relative clauses often hide the main verb. Find the main subject + main verb first.
Reference: Advanced Grammar in Use Units 53–55 · English Grammar in Use Units 92–96

Writers often drop “who is / which is / that are” and keep only the -ing or -ed form. This is very common in DSE Part B2 and newspaper English.

ACTIVE meaning (-ing)

The people living next door come from Italy.

= who are living next door

PASSIVE meaning (-ed)

The weapon used in the murder has been found.

= that was used in the murder

How to “expand” them back (useful for understanding):

See an -ing / -ed phrase after a noun? Mentally insert “who/which + be” and check if it makes sense.

These usually appear at the beginning or end of a sentence (often with a comma) and answer “When?”, “Why?”, “How?” or “Under what condition?”.

Opening her eyes, the baby began to cry. (= When she opened…)

Faced with a bill for $10,000, Ivan took an extra job. (= Because he was faced…)

Having finished the book, I had a holiday. (= After / Because I had finished…)

Warning for students (dangling participle):

The implied subject of the participle should be the same as the subject of the main clause. Wrong: Waiting for the bus, a car splashed me. (The car was not waiting!)

Reference: Advanced Grammar in Use Units 58–59

A whole clause acts like a noun. Very common after verbs of thinking, saying, knowing, and as the subject of a sentence.

As Object

Researchers found that the new drug reduced symptoms.

As Subject

What the students need is more practice with long sentences.

In academic and news texts (DSE Part B2), passive is frequent. It often hides the “doer” and focuses on the action or result.

The policy was introduced by the government last year after public pressure had been ignored for months.

When you see “was / were / has been / had been / is being + V-ed”, treat the whole phrase as one meaning unit (the action happened to the subject).

From Reading to Writing

Once you can see the structure, you can use the same patterns to upgrade your own writing (especially useful for Level 4+ in Paper 2).

Upgrade a simple sentence

Start with a basic idea, then add the structures you just learned.

Ready-to-use patterns for Paper 2

Define a term precisely

Digital detox, which refers to a period of time when a person refrains from using electronic devices, has become popular among young people.

Give reason with participle

Faced with increasing pressure from parents and teachers, many students sacrifice sleep for extra study hours.

Show sequence / result

Having completed the research project, the team presented their findings to the school board.

Add extra information (non-defining)

Online learning, which became widespread during the pandemic, continues to offer flexibility for many learners.

Noun clause as object (reporting ideas)

Recent studies have shown that regular physical activity improves concentration in class.

Tip: In your own writing, aim for 1–2 well-controlled complex structures per paragraph rather than many short simple sentences. Markers notice range and control.

Quick upgrade table (Level 3 → Level 4+)

Basic (Level 3 style) Upgraded with structure
Students feel stressed. They have too much homework. Faced with heavy homework loads, many students feel stressed.
There is a problem. The problem is serious. There is a problem which is becoming increasingly serious.
I finished my work. Then I went home. Having finished my work, I went home.
The government made a plan. The plan helps the poor. The government made a plan that aims to help the poor.
